Introduction

Who was Pazuzu?

Pazuzu was the king of the wind demons in Assyrian and Babylonian belief, and the son of the god Hanbi. He was linked to the southwest wind, which people associated with drought, famine, and locusts.

He was also unusual among demons: people did not only fear him, they invited him in. His fearsome face was used to scare off an even more dangerous demon, Lamashtu.

02
Effects

A danger and a defender

As lord of the winds, Pazuzu stood for the harsh weather that could wreck a harvest. For farming communities, a bad wind season meant hunger.

But his reputation also made him useful. Families facing the dangers of childbirth and infancy turned to him because he was an enemy of Lamashtu, the demon blamed for harming mothers and babies.

A monster frightening enough to scare other monsters.

04
Rituals

Using his power

  1. i.
    Wearing and hanging amulets Pazuzu heads were worn or hung near people thought to be at risk, especially mothers and infants.
  2. ii.
    Placing figures in the home Statuettes were kept in houses as guardians, so the demon's presence would block intruders.
  3. iii.
    Incantations against Lamashtu Spoken rituals drove Lamashtu away, with Pazuzu's image and name giving the words force.
