Introduction

Who was {{ page.title }}?

Pazuzu was an ancient Mesopotamian demon associated with destructive winds and storms, and was considered the king of the "evil wind-demons."5 He originated around 1000 BCE, and was associated with opposing Lamashtu. Pazuzu was not a protective spirit who lent his aid to the āšipu.6 Instead, incantations involving Pazuzu were told from Pazuzu's point of view.7 Unlike benevolent forces, Pazuzu's nature was not altered in his incantations: his destructive nature itself is directed against Lamashtu.8 His protection against Lamashtu is ultimately a side effect.9 Additionally, Pazuzu had power over lesser demons, such as Namtar.10

Trivia
  1. Pazuzu only appeared in artistic representations (like his statues) and in incantations. He is not mentioned in any literary texts. Tiamet is the opposite: she only existed in literary texts and had no incantations.18 Konstantopoulos, "They Are Seven," 4–5.
  2. Pazuzu was a "bigger bully" than Lamashtu, and his "terrifying visage" meant to keep evil away.19Beltz, "Namtar," 11.
